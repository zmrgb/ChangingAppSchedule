ENGLISH 11 · VOCAB LESSONS 1–2 0/20 solid

20 words · 1 session · about 50 minutes

Douglass to the Alamo

Ten words from Frederick Douglass’s lecture hall, ten from the frontier trail to the Alamo. This session walks you through them in the order your memory works best: guess, picture, understand, recall, then practice in the exact quiz format.

The session

    Why this order works

    Retrieval beats rereading

    Every time you pull a word out of memory, the path to it gets stronger. Rereading only feels like learning.

    Pictures + places

    Your brain stores images and locations far better than definitions. Each word gets both.

    Spacing

    Missed words come back fast; known words come back later. Short sessions spread over days beat one long cram.

    Practice like the test

    The mock quizzes use the real format, so quiz day feels like the tenth time, not the first.

    Step 1 · Warm-up · 3 min

    Guess first, learn second

    Pick what each word means. Guess even if you’re not sure; guessing first makes the right answer stick better once you see it. Keys 1–5 answer, Enter goes on.

    Step 2 · Memory Walk · 10 min

    Walk the path from Douglass Hall to the Alamo

    Each word lives at its own spot on the path. At every stop: say the word out loud, read the hook, then close your eyes for 3 seconds and picture the scene: big, loud, moving and weird. Weird images stick. Then switch to Recall walk and name each spot from memory.

    Drag to look around · tap a sign · ← → to walk

    Step 3 · Word Forms · 5 min

    How is the word used in the sentence?

    Every blank is a slot that only one kind of word fits. Look at the words just before and after the blank, and the slot tells you the part of speech before you even think about meaning.

    Slot rules

    If you see…You needExample

    Endings give it away

    noun-ity -acy -ion -ness -ure -ist -ogue -ope -oryferocity, intricacy, introspection, terseness, conjecture, protagonist, prologue, misanthrope, allegory
    adjective-ous -ive -al -ic -able -ate -uteambiguous, provocative, rhetorical, misanthropic, despicable, intricate, dissolute
    verb-ate -ed -ing, or after “to”accentuate(d), usurp(ed), provoke(d)
    adverb-lytersely, ferociously, ambiguously

    Two words wear two hats: vernacular (noun or adjective) and conjecture (noun or verb).

    Step 4 · Clue Method · 6 min

    Put on your SPECS

    Run these five steps on every blank. After a few rounds it becomes automatic, and you’ll see the answer hiding in the sentence.

    1. SScan

      Read the whole paragraph first. What’s the topic? Is the mood positive or negative?

    2. PPart of speech

      Look left and right of the blank. Does it need a noun, verb, or adjective? What form?

    3. EEvidence

      Find the clue words: or, not / but, for example, because, or nearby hint words.

    4. CCreate

      Say your own word for the blank before you look at the bank. “Something like… unclear.”

    5. SSwap & check

      Match it to a bank word, cross it off, then reread the sentence with it in place.

    Step 5 · Drill · 10 min

    Pull every word out of your head

    Each word has to be answered right twice: once by choosing, once by typing it from its definition, spelled correctly. Miss one and it comes back in two cards. The drill ends when all 20 are solid.

    Step 6 · Practice Quiz · 10 min each

    The real format: 4 paragraphs, 20 blanks

    Each paragraph has its own 5-word bank, and every word gets used once. Use SPECS. Fill the blanks you’re sure of first, then use elimination for the rest. Tap mode: tap a blank, then tap a word. Type mode: the bank shows base words and you type the exact form, spelling included.

    Step 7 · Lock It In · 3 min now, then spaced

    Empty your head onto the page

    Close your eyes and walk the path from Douglass Hall to the Alamo in your head. Type every word you meet, in any order, spelling counts. This “brain dump” is one of the strongest memory boosters there is.

    Review schedule

    1. Right now

      Finish this session. Look at your weak words below one more time.

    2. Tonight, before bed

      Run the Drill once (about 8 min) and do the brain dump again. Memory gets locked in while you sleep, so studying right before bed pays off.

    3. Tomorrow morning

      Do the Recall walk in the palace (5 min), then one practice set in Type mode.

    4. 1 hour before the quiz

      Brain dump from memory, then take the practice set you scored lowest on. No new studying; just recall.

    Can’t make every slot? Keep the tonight and morning-of ones. Spreading reviews out is what makes them work.

    On quiz day, for each paragraph

    1. Read the whole paragraph before filling anything in (Scan).
    2. Fill the sure ones first and mentally cross those words off the bank.
    3. For each remaining blank, run P-E-C: part of speech → clue → your own word.
    4. Five words, five blanks: the last word goes in the last blank, but check that it fits.
    5. Reread the finished paragraph. Every sentence should sound right out loud in your head.
    6. If you type answers: check the spelling and the ending (-ed, -s, -ity, -ic).

    Teach it

    Explain 5 of the words to someone, using the hooks. Teaching forces you to rebuild the memory, which makes it stronger.
